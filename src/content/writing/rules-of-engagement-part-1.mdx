*Part one of two.*

I am reading *The American Hagwon* by Min Jee Lin. The book opens with one short line: "The rules of engagement have changed already." I stopped there and read it again. Then I put the book down for a while.

The line stuck with me because it gave a name to something I have felt for months. The world did not change in one big moment. It changed quietly, while most of us were busy doing what we were told would work. By the time we noticed, the new rules were already in place.

## What used to be a given

For a long time, a few things did the talking for you. A good degree said you were skilled. A good employer said you were safe. A job title said you could be trusted. You did your work well, and the system noticed you.

Those things still matter. But they no longer vouch for you on their own. Now you have to show the proof yourself. And everyone else is doing the same.

## Where I see it

Look at how people find jobs. The old way was outbound. You saw a vacancy, you sent your CV, and you waited. More and more, the good jobs come inbound. People who have a public portfolio, who share their thinking and who belong to the right community get a message from a company before any vacancy is posted. Senior people always got hired this way. What is new is that everyone is now expected to play this game.

Look at politicians. The mayor of a big city or the prime minister of a country cannot only do the job. They also have to show the job, every day, on social media. Politicians always cared about their image. But now there is no newspaper in the middle. It is constant and direct, and those who do not keep up fall behind.

Look at education. In India, a good degree was the ladder. A good college led to a software job, and a software job could lead to a career in the West. That ladder is shaky now, because AI and geopolitics both weakened it. A degree from a top institute still opens doors. But a student from a second or third tier college now needs a portfolio on top of the degree. The degree is the entry ticket, not the finish line.

Look at work itself. During COVID, companies hired fast. Then they fired fast. The same thing happened with the AI hiring wave. Now AI makes teams more productive, and large companies cut thousands of jobs at a time. The old deal was loyalty in return for security. That deal is gone. You are a one-person business now, whether your contract says so or not.

And it is not only office work. Publishers ask new authors how many followers they have. College athletes in the US now earn money from their personal brand, so a player with a big following can earn more than a better teammate. In Ukraine, cheap drones changed how wars are fought, and expensive tanks lost their edge almost overnight. That is the rules of engagement changing in the most literal sense.

## The rules keep moving

Here is the part that bothers me most. The old gatekeepers did not disappear. New ones took their place. Platforms like LinkedIn now decide who gets seen. So people start to build what is easy to show, instead of what takes years to learn. If I have one free hour, the honest question is this: do I spend it going deeper in my field, or making something I can post?

And the platforms change too. Websites spent twenty years learning to rank on Google. Now they are learning to show up in AI answers. The rules did not change once. They keep changing.

## Who loses

When I think about who struggles in this new world, it is not the people without talent. It is the people without a community. They got the degree, stayed loyal and kept their heads down. Nobody told them the game had changed.

That is why the word "already" in that first line hits so hard. The rules have *already* changed. Most of us were the last to know.

So what do we do about it? That is part two.
